    What is image compression?

    Image compression makes picture files smaller so pages load faster, emails send quicker and phones use less storage and data. A photo straight from a phone camera is often 3–8 MB; for a website or social media post, a file of 200–500 KB usually looks the same.

    Lossy vs lossless compression

    Lossy compression (JPG and WebP) removes detail the eye barely notices, which gives the biggest savings. The quality setting controls how much is removed. Lossless compression (PNG optimisation) stores exactly the same pixels more efficiently, so nothing changes visually, but the savings are smaller. Reducing a PNG to fewer colours is a lossy step that can make graphics dramatically smaller.

    JPG, PNG and WebP compared

    • JPG / JPEG compressor: best for photos. Works on every device and app. No transparency.
    • PNG compressor: best for logos, icons, screenshots and anything with sharp edges, text or transparency.
    • WebP compressor: a modern format that is typically 25–35% smaller than JPG at the same quality, with transparency support. Ideal for websites.

    Which quality setting should I use?

    • 85–95%: print, portfolios and photos where detail matters.
    • 70–85%: websites, blogs, online shops and social media. The best all-round choice.
    • 40–70%: thumbnails, email attachments and previews where size matters most.

    Resizing is often the biggest win: a 4000-pixel-wide photo shown on a website at 1200 pixels wastes over 90% of its pixels.

    Compress images without losing quality

    To keep your photos looking sharp, resize them to the size you really need, use a quality of 75–85%, and compare the result with the original using the before-and-after slider. For graphics and screenshots, keep PNG and use lossless optimisation. If a file is already well optimised, the tool tells you that no size reduction was achieved rather than pretending.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is this image compressor free?

    Yes. Compress as many JPG, PNG and WebP images as you like and download them individually or as a ZIP. There is no sign-up, no watermark and no daily limit.

    Are my images uploaded to a server?

    No. Your images are compressed by your own browser on your device. They are never uploaded to SocialTush or any other service, and they are gone when you close the page.

    How do I compress images without losing quality?

    For photos, a JPG or WebP quality of 75–85% gives much smaller files that look the same to the eye. For PNG graphics, use lossless optimisation, which keeps every pixel identical. Resizing very large photos to the size you actually need saves the most space.

    Why is my compressed file bigger than the original?

    Some images are already well compressed. Re-encoding them at a high quality, or converting a small graphic to another format, can produce a larger file. The tool tells you honestly when this happens; try a lower quality, WebP, or resizing.

    Which format should I choose?

    WebP gives the smallest files for websites and is supported by all modern browsers. JPG is best for photos that must work everywhere. PNG is best for logos, screenshots and images that need transparency.

    Why does the quality slider not change PNG files?

    PNG is a lossless format, so it has no quality setting. The tool optimises PNGs losslessly, or you can reduce the number of colours for much smaller files, which is a lossy change.

    Can I compress many images at once?

    Yes. Select or drop several images, compress them in one go and download them all as a single ZIP file.
